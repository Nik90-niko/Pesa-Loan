How to Check if a Digital Lender Is Licensed in Kenya

A practical guide to protecting yourself before borrowing money online.

Digital loans have become increasingly accessible in Kenya. Many people can apply for credit using a smartphone without visiting a bank. However, the growth of digital lending has also made it important for borrowers to verify the companies they deal with.

Before submitting your identification details, sharing personal information, or accepting a loan, take time to confirm that the lender is genuine and understand the terms of the service.

Why You Should Verify a Digital Lender

Checking a lender's legitimacy can help you avoid fraudulent services, misleading loan offers, hidden charges, and misuse of personal information.

A legitimate lending business should clearly identify the company behind its service and provide information about its products, customer support, and applicable terms.

What Is the Central Bank of Kenya?

The Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) is the country's central bank. It regulates various financial institutions and oversees certain categories of financial services providers.

Digital credit providers operating within the regulatory framework applicable to them may be required to obtain authorization or licensing. Borrowers should check the latest regulatory information to determine whether a provider is appropriately authorized.

How to Check a Digital Lender

1. Search for the lender's official name

Look for the company's complete legal name, not only the name displayed on its mobile application. A business may operate under a brand name that differs from its registered name.

Check the company's website, contact information, and business details. Be cautious if you cannot identify the company responsible for providing the loan.

2. Check the regulator's information

Visit the official Central Bank of Kenya website and look for information about licensed digital credit providers and relevant regulatory notices.

Always use official regulator sources rather than relying solely on social media posts or advertisements claiming that a lender is licensed.

3. Confirm that the name matches

Compare the company name shown on the lender's website and application with the name appearing in official regulatory information. Do not assume that two companies are the same simply because their names look similar.

4. Check the official application

Download applications through trusted app stores or access them through the lender's official website. Avoid downloading APK files from unfamiliar websites or messages.

5. Read the loan agreement

Before accepting credit, read the terms carefully. Check the amount you will receive, fees, interest, repayment date, late-payment consequences, and total amount payable.

Warning Signs of Loan Scams

Be careful if a loan service:

  • Promises guaranteed approval to everyone.
  • Requests an upfront payment to release a loan.
  • Uses an unofficial phone number or suspicious website.
  • Does not identify the company behind the service.
  • Requests your M-Pesa PIN or banking password.
  • Pressures you to accept a loan immediately.
  • Uses threats before you have even received a loan.
  • Claims to be licensed but cannot be independently verified.
Important: Never share your M-Pesa PIN, passwords, or one-time security codes with a loan agent. A request for an upfront payment should be independently verified before you send any money.

Protect Your Personal Information

Loan applications may request personal information to assess eligibility. Only provide information through verified official channels.

Review application permissions and privacy policies. Be cautious about granting access to information that does not appear necessary for the service being offered.

If you suspect your information is being misused, keep records of messages, calls, payment requests, and other evidence.

What to Do If You Encounter a Suspicious Lender

  1. Do not send money to unlock a loan.
  2. Do not share your PIN, password, or verification codes.
  3. Stop communicating through suspicious links.
  4. Save screenshots and relevant messages.
  5. Verify the company through official sources.
  6. Report suspected fraud through the appropriate official channels.

Can a Licensed Lender Still Offer an Expensive Loan?

Licensing alone does not mean that a loan is affordable or suitable for you. You must still review the loan's cost, repayment schedule, and conditions.

Compare different offers and calculate how the repayments will affect your income and essential expenses.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if a loan app is genuine in Kenya?

Check the company's identity, official website, regulatory information where applicable, application source, customer support details, and loan agreement.

Should I pay a registration fee before receiving a loan?

Be cautious of upfront payment requests. Verify any fees directly with the lender through its official contact channels before paying.

Can a loan app access my contacts?

Application permissions depend on the software and its design. Review permissions before granting access and understand how the provider says it handles your personal information.

What should I do if a lender threatens me?

Keep records of the communication. If you believe the conduct is unlawful or threatening, consider reporting it to the appropriate authorities.

Conclusion

Verifying a digital lender before borrowing is an important step toward protecting your money and personal information.

Take time to confirm the lender's identity, check relevant regulatory information, read the loan agreement, and understand the total repayment amount. Avoid rushed decisions and borrow only when repayment is realistic.
